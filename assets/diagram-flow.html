Tizen Lite · what runs where

Why it needs a small computer, not a server The TV can’t keep root across a power-off — so something on your LAN re-applies it once per boot

1 · on power-on
Small always-on box
A Raspberry Pi, NAS or the PC already on your LAN. Home network only.
→
2 · ~20 s
Attaches & roots
Connects over sdb, runs the exploit, verifies uid 0.
→
3 · ~40 s
Applies the state
Masks units, disables records, kills the heavy apps, puts your launcher up.
→
4 · done
Slim TV
Root is released. Nothing stays running on the TV.
The honest answer
A server? No. A host? Yes.
Nothing is ever sent to the internet and no cloud service is involved. But the dull part — the service masks and the process kills — lives in RAM and dies at power-off, so it has to be re-applied on each boot. That's the small box's only job, and it takes about a minute.
Without that box
  • App removals stay removed — those are files and database rows, written once
  • Service masks revert — the TV comes back bloated after a power cycle
  • Art Mode & co. come back — the kill-list needs a run after boot
No cloud · no account · no telemetry from this tool · the TV itself receives nothing it didn’t already have
